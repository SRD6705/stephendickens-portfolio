Project Brief

THINK Design ran a competitive usability study for UiPath, evaluating its RPA (robotic process automation) developer tool against two competing platforms. The goal was a SWOT-level understanding of UiPath's developer experience relative to the field: not just whether it was good, but specifically where it won, where it was vulnerable, and what to fix next.

My Role

I was the lead researcher on this study for THINK Design. I designed the moderated study protocol, facilitated participant sessions, and synthesized the cross-tool findings into the SWOT analysis and prioritized recommendations delivered to UiPath's product team.

Approach

  • Ran remote, one-on-one moderated sessions on Zoom; each participant worked in their assigned tool using a think-aloud protocol
  • Recruited 36 developers from 210 applicants (via job boards, community forums, and social channels), screened and tiered by experience level (beginner, intermediate, advanced), 12 per tool
  • Ran 4 standardized tasks per session (create, update, and share an automation), identical across all three tools for direct comparison
  • Collected post-session ratings across usability, usefulness, credibility, and desirability
  • Synthesized a cross-tool SWOT analysis, grounded in verbatim participant quotes

Key Findings

100% task completion: UiPath had the highest completion rate on every single task, including full completion on Task 1, versus under 60% for the closest competitor.
Most positive ratings for UiPath across usability, usefulness, and desirability, ahead of both competing tools.
One real opportunity: a competitor's newly released version actually rated higher on visual attractiveness, a specific, addressable gap for UiPath's interface.
The study's only 1/5 rating went to a competitor, tied to unfamiliarity with a recently released version, a reminder that new-version confusion can tank scores independent of underlying product quality.

Recommendations

  • Offer multiple workbench views (list vs. flow view) to match different developers' mental models
  • Simplify orchestrator setup and troubleshooting: several participants needed a full system restart just to debug
  • Add contextual help directly inside the workflow builder, not just in external documentation
  • Close the visual gap between the product's interface and the broader brand's visual language
