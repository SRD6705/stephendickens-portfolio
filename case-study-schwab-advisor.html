Ten features, limited capacity, no shared evidence

The Schwab Advisor Platform had 10 potential new features and limited capacity to build them. Before committing design and development resources, the product team needed to know which features mattered most to the financial advisors who would use them, and then how the most valuable ones should fit into an advisor's workflow. I designed and ran a two-part study to answer both questions: a quantitative ranking, followed by a qualitative check on design direction.

A high-stakes moment for independent advisors

Schwab Advisor Services provides the infrastructure that independent Registered Investment Advisors (RIAs) use to manage their clients' accounts, including trading and operational support. The digital onboarding platform supports one of an advisor's most time-sensitive and stressful moments: telling clients they are going independent and getting them into the Schwab system.

Why adoption was stalling

  • Adoption: Restrictive document capabilities were keeping advisors from using the full platform
  • Advisor impact: Advisors were forced into workarounds or avoided the product altogether
  • Business consequences: Lost revenue and efficiency, including the cost of advisors relying on other tools
  • Alignment: Stakeholders held competing views on what the roadmap should prioritize
"What advisors have said in the past is, we love your product, but we can't use it because we can't attach our contractual forms or our marketing material that we want to include in the digital package."

Product manager, describing advisor feedback

My Role

  • Survey design, including feature definitions, MaxDiff structure, and screening criteria
  • Launching the survey and monitoring response quality
  • Analysis and synthesis of the quantitative results
  • Planning, moderating, and synthesizing the usability study
  • Presenting combined findings and recommendations to product and design partners

Quant decided which, qual shaped how

  • Inputs: Reviewed behavioral analytics and findings from prior usability studies to shape the feature list and the survey design
  • Quantitative: Ran a MaxDiff survey with 200+ financial advisors to prioritize the 10 features. I built the structure from scratch by adapting UserZoom's stack-ranking question type, so advisors repeatedly chose the most-wanted and least-wanted feature from small sets. Each advisor saw 15 sets of four features, with every feature appearing six times and compared against the others. MaxDiff forces tradeoffs between features, rather than asking advisors to rate each one in isolation
  • Analysis: Used Hierarchical Bayes estimation to generate individual-level utility scores for each feature, then converted them into relative importance scores to rank all 10 features
  • Triangulation: Compared the results against existing behavioral data to check where stated preferences and actual usage aligned
  • Qualitative: Followed with a low-fidelity usability study with advisors to evaluate design direction for the top features
  • Synthesis: Combined the ranking and the usability findings into one readout for product and design

Ten became three, before development started

Three priority features. The MaxDiff results narrowed the team's focus from 10 candidate features to three.
Evidence that challenged assumptions. The ranking gave the product owner evidence of what advisors wanted, which ran counter to what other stakeholders expected the priorities to be. With competing opinions in the room, the ranking gave everyone one shared, quantitative reference point.
Workflow fit before development. The usability study showed how advisors expected those features to fit into their workflow, which shaped the design direction before anything moved into development.
A redirected roadmap. Product used the evidence to defend its roadmap decisions and shift development effort toward the features most likely to drive platform adoption.

What I'd Do Differently

Reflection

The results ran counter to what some stakeholders expected, which is exactly when evidence needs to be trusted by everyone, not just the product owner. If I ran this again, I'd bring the stakeholders most likely to disagree into the feature definitions earlier, so the ranking carried their trust as well as the product owner's.
